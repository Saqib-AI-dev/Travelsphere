Faisal Mosque

Islamabad's Architectural Masterpiece – The Largest Mosque in South Asia

Plan Your Visit

About Faisal Mosque

Faisal Mosque, officially known as Shah Faisal Mosque, is the national mosque of Pakistan and one of the largest mosques in the world. Located at the foothills of the Margalla Hills in Islamabad, its contemporary design with eight sloping concrete shells resembling a Bedouin tent makes it an architectural marvel.

Quick Facts

Location: Islamabad, Pakistan

Architect: Vedat Dalokay (Turkey)

Completed: 1986

Capacity: 300,000 worshippers

Funded by: King Faisal of Saudi Arabia

Architectural Highlights

Unique Tent-Shaped Roof

Eight massive concrete shells form a desert tent design.

88-Meter Tall Minarets

Four slender minarets inspired by Turkish architecture.

Massive Prayer Hall

Can accommodate 100,000 people with a stunning chandelier.

Photo Gallery

Best Time to Visit

Anytime of the year – Open daily from dawn to dusk. Best light for photography: early morning or late afternoon.

Avoid prayer times (especially Friday noon) if visiting as a tourist.

How to Reach

  • Location: Shah Faisal Avenue, near Margalla Hills
  • By Car/Taxi: 15-20 minutes from central Islamabad
  • Public Transport: Local buses and vans from F-8 and G-8 sectors

Visitor Information

Dress Code: Modest clothing required (headscarf for women recommended)

Entry: Free for all visitors

Photography: Allowed (respect worshippers)
